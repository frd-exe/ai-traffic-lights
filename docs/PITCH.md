# Pitch

Most traffic lights still run on fixed timers: thirty seconds of green whether the road is jammed or empty. SignalFlow shows what changes when an AI runs the signals instead. In a live split screen, the same simulated city, with the same cars and the same rush hour, runs on fixed timers on the left and on AI control on the right: a Gemini model reads the queues every few seconds and plans which approaches get green, on top of a proven max-pressure controller, while a safety layer guarantees yellow, all-red, minimum green and that no one waits more than a minute. Press "Surge" and both sides get the same traffic spike at the same moment, so the comparison stays fair. If the AI is unavailable or its quota runs out, the junctions fall back to fixed timers through a safe transition, and the screen says so. In our 10-minute simulations under rush-hour demand, the AI cut average waiting time by 13% versus fixed timers (max-pressure alone by 23%). Results vary a lot between seeds, and everything is simulated, so treat these as indicative, not measured. The point is to make the trade-offs visible and testable before anyone touches a real intersection.
